Monitoring, Evaluation & Learning

Learning questions, exact metric definitions, where each number comes from, and the baseline-to-endline evaluation design.

For: M&E / researchLeadership & funderEOC & surveillanceStatus: Draft v0.1Updated: 29 October 2026

In short

  • Four learning questions: Is it faster? Is it more accurate and usable? Do multiple streams add intelligence? Does the GHEC-AI hand-off work?
  • Every indicator has one exact definition, one data source and one owner — most come straight from RADAR's own timestamps and logs.
  • Design: baseline before deployment, routine monitoring, endline near the end; mixed methods; internally led by WSU with KNPHI.
  • Evidence feeds three decisions: gate go/no-go, model recalibration, and scale-up policy.

Why it matters

The gates are judged on numbers. If “concordance” or “detection-to-classification time” are defined loosely, two people can compute different answers from the same data — and a gate can be missed on a technicality or passed without real improvement. Precise definitions agreed now also protect the credibility of the two planned publications.

4Evaluation design

  1. Baseline (before deployment). At the national EOC, reconstruct detection-to-classification, detection-to-escalation and 7-1-7 for recent events from existing records — a Gate 1 milestone that cannot be redone later. Proposal Repeat at each county before its go-live. Recommended
  2. Routine monitoring. Indicators computed automatically from MER and logs; monthly dashboard; quarterly review.
  3. Parallel runs. Eight weeks per site generating concordance datasets (doc 09).
  4. Endline. Toward the end of the grant: the same measures, plus surveys, key-informant interviews and workflow observation. Proposal
  5. Analysis. Pre/post comparison per site; where enough events exist, an interrupted time-series design; qualitative analysis of adoption barriers.
Independence

The formal evaluation is internally led by WSU with KNPHI rather than by an independent third party. Proposal To strengthen credibility: pre-register the protocol and analysis plan, keep the expert panel independent of the build team, have KEMRI lead data-quality assessment, and invite an external reviewer to audit the endline analysis. Recommended

Watch for small numbers

Level 2–3 events are uncommon. At some sites there may be too few real events in a period for stable estimates. Report confidence intervals, pool across sites where appropriate, and supplement with simulation cases — clearly labelled as such.

5Data collection and ethics

  • System-generated data (timestamps, logs) under KNPHI data-custodian approval.
  • Surveys, interviews and observation with informed consent or an approved waiver through the KEMRI ethics process. Proposal
  • Casual research assistants (budgeted in periods 1 and 3) support data collection and quality checks.
