Doc 06·Product & Architecture

Intelligence Layer: Data Fusion & Early Warning

How RADAR turns harmonised feeds into early-warning signals: record linkage, anomaly detection, cross-stream fusion, forecasting and risk scoring.

For: EngineeringM&E / researchEOC & surveillanceStatus: Draft v0.1Updated: 29 October 2026

In short

  • The intelligence layer turns harmonised feeds into signal cards: a risk score plus the evidence behind it, ready for DMT-PHE.
  • It works in stages: baselines → aberration detection → space-time clustering → cross-stream fusion → risk score, with climate forecasts as an extra input.
  • Transparent statistics first, machine learning only where it clearly adds value — every alert must be explainable to an EOC officer.
  • Thresholds are tuned to keep alert burden manageable per county, and recalibrated from confirmed and discarded outcomes.

Why it matters

This is where “predictive risk-based analytics” and “earlier detection” — two of the proposal's outcome claims — are either earned or not. The value comes from fusion: animal and climate signals often appear weeks before human cases, so seeing them together on one event buys time. But a noisy detector that floods EOCs with false alarms would destroy trust faster than any other failure.

What the evidence says about detectors

A comparison of daily syndromic surveillance algorithms (Noufaily et al., Bioinformatics 2019) found no single best method Research:

  • Farrington Flexible had the highest specificity (~0.98) and positive predictive value — fewest false alarms — but lower power for small outbreaks.
  • Negative-binomial regression (UKHSA's RAMMIE) detected outbreaks most often and typically 2–3 days earlier.
  • EARS C1–C3 had markedly lower specificity (0.81–0.92) and are less suited to multi-purpose surveillance.

For RADAR this suggests a pairing: a high-specificity detector to raise alerts, and a high-sensitivity detector to raise “watch” flags that only escalate when another stream agrees — exactly what fusion is for.

3Why fusion buys time

Rift Valley fever is the clearest One Health case: heavy rainfall and flooding create mosquito breeding conditions; livestock abortions and deaths follow; human febrile illness comes later. KABS contributed to detecting the 2018 RVF outbreak in livestock and humans across three counties. Research

Climate-based RVF models in Kenya show both promise and limits: a dynamic model using 30 years of rainfall, temperature, humidity, vegetation index and sea-surface temperature predicted observed events with high skill (Brier scores 0.007–0.022), but explained variance differed sharply by ecology — R² 0.66 in Garissa versus 0.16–0.21 in Murang'a and Kwale. Research Forecasts should therefore act as a prior that raises sensitivity in at-risk areas, not as an alert on their own.

6How detectors are evaluated and tuned

  1. Back-test on history. Replay past seasons (including known outbreaks such as 2018 RVF and recent cholera and mpox) and measure sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value and timeliness.
  2. Set an alert budget. Agree with each EOC how many alerts per week it can realistically review; tune thresholds to stay within it.
  3. Shadow first. Run new detectors silently alongside live ones before they raise alerts.
  4. Learn from outcomes. Every confirmed and discarded event becomes a label for recalibration (doc 12).

7Data realities to design for

  • Reporting artefacts. Training drives, app outages and funding changes move counts as much as disease does. Track reporting completeness alongside cases.
  • Zero vs. missing. A KABS “zero report” is information; a missing week is not. Never treat missing as zero.
  • Delays. Weekly aggregates arrive late and get revised; use nowcasting adjustments or wait windows.
  • Small numbers. Many sub-counties have very low counts; aggregate spatially when needed.
  • Mobility and telecom data are used only where legally and operationally permitted, at minimum granularity, aggregated or de-identified. Proposal

8Open questions

  1. How many years of history can each MVP source provide? Seasonal baselines need several seasons. Owner: data analyst · inventory
  2. What alert budget can the national and county EOCs absorb? Owner: KNPHI PHEOC · MVP
  3. Which historic outbreaks are documented well enough for back-testing? Owner: KNPHI + KEMRI
