• RADAR has five layers on top of existing systems: integration, bridge (MER), intelligence, decision support, and users & channels.
  • Identity, audit and observability cut across every layer.
  • The recommended stack is open source and government-ownable: OpenHIM, HAPI FHIR, PostgreSQL, NATS, Python/R analytics, an on-premise LLM served by vLLM, and Keycloak.
  • RADAR is not a big-data problem — volumes are modest. The hard parts are reliability, integration, audit and safe AI.

The architecture fixes where each responsibility lives, so the connector, analytics, AI and UI teams can work in parallel without stepping on each other. It also has to be something KNPHI can run after October 2029: every choice here favours tools that are well documented, widely used in African health information systems, and free of licence lock-in.

Components and the path of one record

  1. Ingest. A connector pulls the week's KABS reports through the gateway and lands the raw file unchanged.
  2. Validate, map, code. Records are checked against the source schema, mapped to MER/FHIR fields, and coded (syndrome, species, admin unit). Failures go to quarantine.
  3. Link. The linker matches a cluster of livestock abortions to an existing suspected-RVF MER in the same sub-county, or opens a new one.
  4. Publish. The MER service writes the change (with Provenance) and publishes an event on the bus.
  5. Analyse. Analytics workers recompute baselines and fusion scores; a signal card is written with its evidence.
  6. Decide. The rules engine evaluates the RVF trigger card; the agent drafts a cited recommendation; an officer confirms Level 2 in the DMT-PHE workspace.
  7. Act and record. The decision lands in the audit log; the outbound gateway notifies the level owner and sends the GHEC-AI hand-off.

Sizing and hardware

RADAR's data volumes are small by modern standards. Published figures give a sense of scale: KABS received about 11,400 livestock reports in its first two years; one county's community EBS produced about 8,700 signals in a year. Research Even nationally, structured records will be in the low millions over the grant — comfortably handled by a single well-configured PostgreSQL server. The compute-heavy component is LLM inference.

The proposal describes the budgeted server as hosting the RADAR development platform, integrated surveillance databases, analytics engines, AI services and decision-support tools. Two hardware risks to settle early
  • GPU. Running an LLM on-premise needs at least one data-centre GPU (for example a 48 GB-class card for a mid-size open-weight model). Confirm whether the quoted server configuration includes one; if not, budget it or agree a Government-authorised hosted model endpoint. Open
  • Single point of failure. One server hosts everything. Plan a second node or a Government-cloud standby for recovery (see doc 11).

Indicative configuration Recommended: 2 × mid-range Xeon 6 P-core CPUs, 512 GB RAM, 8 × NVMe in RAID 10, mirrored boot, 1–2 × 48 GB-class GPUs, dual power supplies, iDRAC Enterprise. Finalise after a load test of the MVP.

Designing for low connectivity

  • A light web UI that loads quickly on weak links; static assets cached by the browser.
  • Offline-capable forms for county officers, syncing when the link returns, with the officer's original action time preserved.
  • SMS alerts for escalations so no one depends on having the app open.
  • Starlink connectivity at KNPHI and implementation sites is budgeted across all three periods. Proposal
  • A documented paper fallback (the existing DMT-PHE tools) with back-entry when systems return (doc 08).
