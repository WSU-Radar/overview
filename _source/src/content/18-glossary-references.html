RADAR brings together epidemiology, veterinary surveillance, health informatics, law and AI — each with its own vocabulary. A shared glossary keeps meetings and documents precise, especially for terms like “detection”, “notification” and “concordance” that are measured at the gates.

Key terms

TermDefinition used in RADAR
SignalAn initial report that may indicate a public-health event; not yet verified.
EventA verified occurrence of potential public-health concern, represented by one MER.
DetectionThe date an event is first recorded by any source or in any system (7-1-7 definition).
NotificationThe date information reaches the first public-health authority responsible for action.
ClassificationThe first human-confirmed DMT-PHE level for an event.
Escalation levelDMT-PHE Level 1 (sub-county/county), Level 2 (county/national), Level 3 (national/regional surge).
Signal cardRADAR's packaged risk score and evidence for an event, handed to DMT-PHE.
Trigger cardDMT-PHE's per-disease table of case definitions, thresholds, triggers, actions, support and exit criteria.
ConcordanceAgreement between the AI-supported classification and an independent expert-panel reference.
Adverse decision eventA decision influenced by the AI that delayed or wrongly downgraded a response, or caused harm (definition to be finalised).
Parallel runA period in which RADAR and usual practice run side by side and are compared with an expert panel.
Integration level (L0–L4)Maturity of a source connection, from manual upload to MER publish/subscribe.
Minimum necessarySharing only the fields and granularity required for the stated purpose.
Rapid-response / continuous-monitoring modeRADAR's two operating modes: supporting an active high-consequence outbreak, or routinely watching endemic and outbreak-prone diseases.
