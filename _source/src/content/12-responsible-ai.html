• RADAR's AI has one intended use: help EOC officers classify and escalate events consistently and quickly under DMT-PHE — never to decide on its own.
  • Every model version passes an assurance loop: test sets, offline evaluation, simulation and parallel run, monitored live use, recalibration.
  • Key measures: AI–expert concordance, false-negative rate, citation accuracy, SUS, zero adverse decision events — with thresholds rising at each gate.
  • Known AI risks (prompt injection via free text, hallucination, automation bias, drift) each have a named control.

In outbreak response, a confident but wrong recommendation can delay containment (false negative) or waste scarce teams (false positive). WHO's guidance on large multi-modal models warns specifically about automation bias — professionals overlooking errors or handing difficult decisions to the model. Research The gates make AI performance a funding condition; this document says how that performance is earned and kept.

The assurance loop

A new version is any change to the model, prompt, retrieval index, knowledge-base documents or rule thresholds. Each version gets a model card and release notes, and can be rolled back.

How performance is measured

MeasureDefinition Recommended
AI–expert concordanceShare of assessed events where the AI-supported classification and level match the expert-panel reference. Report alongside a chance-corrected statistic (weighted kappa) and by outbreak category and site.
False-negative rateShare of events the panel placed at Level 2–3 that the AI-supported path placed lower. Ceiling agreed at Gate 1 Proposal.
Citation accuracyShare of cited passages that exist and support the claim (sampled and checked by reviewers).
Unsupported-claim rateShare of recommendation statements with no supporting citation.
Override rateShare of recommendations modified or overridden, with reasons — a learning signal, not a failure measure.
Adverse decision eventA decision influenced by the AI that delayed or wrongly downgraded a response, or caused harm. Any one is reviewed as a safety incident.
System Usability ScaleTen-item questionnaire scored 0–100; 68 is average, scores above about 80 are in the top 10%. Research

Test sets and the expert panel

  • Golden set: the 33 simulated interactions from the March 2026 evaluation, the five national SIMEX scenarios, and documented historical events (e.g. RVF 2018, recent cholera and mpox), each with a panel-agreed reference. Held out from any tuning.
  • Adversarial set: ambiguous presentations, conflicting streams, missing data, injection attempts, Kiswahili and mixed-language text.
  • Expert panel: at least three experienced epidemiologists/EOC leads not involved in building the tool; blinded; disagreements resolved by a pre-set rule (e.g. majority, then discussion).

Monitoring, incidents and the kill switch

  • Published error and override log — a Gate 2 milestone, with a documented benchmarking and recalibration procedure. Proposal
  • Incident reporting: any suspected adverse decision event is logged, reviewed within 5 working days by KNPHI and UM/CGHE, and reported at the next steering committee.
  • Kill switch: KNPHI can switch the agent off instantly; the workspace continues in rules-only mode with full evidence.
  • Retraining: AI model retraining using national EOC operational data is planned in Phase 2 Proposal — every retrained version re-enters the loop at step 3.
