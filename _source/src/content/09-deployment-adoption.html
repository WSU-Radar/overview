• RADAR rolls out to the national EOC first (Gate 1), then to five priority county EOCs (Gate 2).
  • Every site follows the same seven steps: readiness, ICT & access, training, an eight-week parallel run, go/no-go, live use, support.
  • The parallel run produces the concordance dataset by comparing RADAR and usual practice against a blinded expert panel.
  • Adoption is designed in: champions, less (not more) work for officers, fast support, and refreshers to counter staff turnover.

A technically excellent RADAR that county officers do not use fails every gate: SUS, documentation completeness and 7-1-7 all depend on real use. The JEE scored activation and coordination of personnel at the lowest level; uneven county readiness after devolution and frequent staff rotation are named gaps. Proposal Deployment and adoption need as much design as the software.

Choosing the five counties

Selection criteria to agree with KNPHI Recommended:

  • Outbreak burden across the four categories (e.g. cholera, RVF, VHF risk).
  • One Health relevance — pastoral and wildlife interfaces where animal signals matter.
  • Cross-border exposure — points of entry, neighbouring outbreaks.
  • Readiness — functioning county EOC, KABS and EBS coverage, connectivity, leadership commitment.
  • Overlap with DMT-PHE pilots — CGP reports that DMT-PHE pilot testing was planned in ten high-risk counties. Research
  • Diversity — include at least one county with weaker infrastructure so the design is tested where it is hardest.

The eight-week parallel run

The proposal and stage gates require an eight-week parallel validation deployment of DMT-PHE at the national EOC, generating a concordance dataset against independent expert-panel review. Proposal Design points Recommended:

  • Blinding. Panel members see the same facts but not the AI or usual-practice answer. For the first weeks, keep the AI recommendation hidden from decision-makers (shadow mode) so the comparison is clean; reveal it once the protocol allows.
  • Enough cases. Real Level 2–3 events in eight weeks may be few. Pre-agree a minimum number of assessed events and top up with structured simulation cases and retrospective events so the concordance estimate is meaningful.
  • Pre-registered analysis. Define concordance (exact level agreement, plus a chance-corrected statistic such as weighted kappa), timeliness and documentation completeness before the run starts (doc 14).
  • Weekly discrepancy review. Every disagreement is reviewed by KNPHI and UM/CGHE; safety concerns stop the clock.
  • County runs repeat this in Phase 2, with AI retraining on national operational data in between.

Adoption by design

Less work, not more

RADAR must remove re-typing and phone reconstruction. Measure time-on-task; cut any step that adds burden.

Site champions

One trained champion per EOC, first line of support and feedback channel.

Closing the loop

Show officers what happened to their signals and how their overrides improved the system.

Official backing

Update KNPHI SOPs so RADAR is the recognised record, not an optional extra.
