• DMT-PHE is KNPHI's standard framework for deciding when and how far to escalate a public-health event: trigger cards, three escalation levels, support packages and 7-1-7-aligned accountability.
  • RADAR digitises it and adds an AI agent that gathers evidence, drafts a recommendation and cites the guideline pages behind it.
  • The rule is “LLM proposes, rules verify, a human decides”: the escalation level comes from deterministic trigger-card rules, and nothing takes effect until a named officer confirms, modifies or overrides it.
  • Every step is logged in an auditable, time-stamped record.

The JEE scored activation and coordination of health personnel in an emergency at 1 of 5 — its lowest score — and escalation has relied on informal discussion rather than a structured framework. Staff rotation and infrequent events mean protocols are hard to apply consistently in real time. A digitised, evidence-fed DMT-PHE makes the same decision for the same facts every time — and the AI agent makes the right guidance available in seconds rather than hours. Getting the safety design right is also what Gates 1–3 test most directly.

DMT-PHE today

KNPHI's Decision-Making Tool for Public Health Emergencies was developed with technical support from CGP and the TDDAP2 programme, validated at a national workshop in October 2025 (40+ participants, five simulation exercises), and launched in March 2026 as the primary escalation interface within the PHEOC. How the AI agent works

The agent draws on an expert-curated knowledge base — the DMT-PHE user guides, MoH-approved national and international outbreak guidelines, and relevant scientific literature. To prevent hallucinations it cites specific documents and pages and shows a transparent reasoning trace; the responsible EOC officer confirms or overrides each recommendation, with the justification logged. Managing the knowledge base

  • A document register: title, version, approving body, effective date, status (approved / superseded).
  • Only approved, current documents are indexed; superseded versions are removed from retrieval but kept for audit.
  • Chunks keep their page anchors so citations point to a real page a reviewer can open.
  • Hybrid retrieval (keyword + vector) so exact phrases like case definitions are found reliably.

The DMT-PHE workspace

What officers see. Recommended

  1. Inbox of signal cards sorted by risk band and time waiting, filtered to the officer's county or national view.
  2. Event page: timeline, map, linked records, evidence per stream, data caveats.
  3. Recommendation panel: rules level, agent draft, citations (click to open the page), missing information.
  4. Decision: confirm, modify or override — a reason is mandatory for modify/override, chosen from a short list plus free text.
  5. Actions: the 7-1-7 checklist with due times; GHEC-AI request when authorised.

Timers: if a high-band signal card is not reviewed within an agreed time, the supervisor is alerted; if a Level 2–3 decision is pending, KNPHI PHEOC is alerted. Escalation should never depend on someone happening to open the app.

From prototype to production

  • A digitised DMT-PHE and an agentic version, developed by the consortium, already exist; the prototype (dmt-agent.streamlit.app) was tested on PHEIUE, RVF and mpox scenarios and benchmarked to 7-1-7 and IHR Annex 2. Proposal
  • University of Michigan (CGHE) leads the agentic decision-support architecture, guardrails, containerisation and maintenance; WSU leads integration with the MER and connectors (doc 15).
  • Production keeps the prototype's prompts and evaluation set as a baseline, and moves the code into the RADAR stack with the validator, audit log and access control.
