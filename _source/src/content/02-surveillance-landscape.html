• Outbreak signals in Kenya are spread across six sectors and more than a dozen systems, each with its own owner, format and rhythm.
  • RADAR connects to them in priority order: four sources for the MVP, the rest in Phases 1–2.
  • Every source needs the same things before it connects: a custodian, a legal basis and agreement, an access route, and known identifiers and timestamps.
  • This document is the seed of the dataset inventory — a Gate 1 deliverable.

The MER (doc 03) can only link what it can reach. Knowing exactly which system holds which piece of an outbreak — and how good, how fast and how accessible that data is — decides what the MVP can realistically show and which agreements must be signed first.

The landscape at a glance

Kenya's ecosystem is expansive and increasingly digital, but each system carries its own data. Events that arise across more than one system — which is where the most dangerous outbreaks originate — are the ones most likely to be missed or reported late. Proposal

The gap RADAR is built for

The EBS evaluation explicitly names “no unique identifiers linking lab results to reported events” as a challenge. That is exactly the gap the Minimum Event Record closes. KABS also contributed to detecting the 2018 RVF outbreak across three counties — evidence that animal signals carry early warning value worth fusing with human data.

  • Reporting fluctuates. CEBS reporting rose sharply and then declined; baselines must handle reporting artefacts, not just disease trends (see doc 06).
  • Verification is the bottleneck. Signals went uninvestigated when officers lacked transport funds — RADAR can make this visible, but the fix is operational.
  • Different geographies and codes. Facility codes (KMHFL), administrative units and GPS points must be harmonised before linking.

How sources are prioritised

A source joins the MVP only if it scores well on all five criteria. Recommended

  1. Signal value for the MVP's priority events (see the matrix above).
  2. Access readiness — an API, export or LATTICE route exists today.
  3. Agreement readiness — the custodian is willing and a data-sharing agreement or MOU can be signed in time for Gate 1.
  4. Data quality — completeness and timeliness are good enough to build a baseline.
  5. Linkability — the source carries place, time and syndrome fields that the MER can match on.

Applying these gives the recommended MVP set: KHIS2 (IDSR), m-Dharura, KABS and the EOC/DMT-PHE decision log. Together they cover the human–animal link for RVF and the human-only pathway for cholera, and they produce the timestamps needed for the 7-1-7 baseline.
