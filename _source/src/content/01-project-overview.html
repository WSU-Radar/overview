• RADAR is an AI-assisted integration and decision layer embedded in Kenya's Emergency Operations Centres (EOCs) at KNPHI and in five priority counties.
  • It joins signals about the same outbreak from human, animal, environmental and laboratory systems into one shared event record, scores the risk, and routes a structured recommendation into the digitised DMT-PHE — where a person confirms or overrides it.
  • It does not replace existing systems, and it never escalates on its own.
  • Success = faster, more consistent, measurable detection-to-response, judged against the 7-1-7 target.

Kenya is data-rich but insight-limited: the facts about one outbreak sit in five different places under five different IDs. Nobody can say, without rebuilding it by hand, when an event was detected, notified or acted on — so delays are invisible and decisions inconsistent.

This document is the shared starting point: every other document assumes the problem, scope and outcomes defined here.

The problem RADAR solves

Kenya's human health surveillance runs under IDSR, with reporting spread across KHIS2, eCHIS and event-based surveillance (m-Dharura). Animal and wildlife reporting runs on KABS and EarthRanger; climate and drought data sit with KMD and NDMA; laboratory results sit in laboratory information systems; and response decisions sit in EOC spreadsheets and paper logs. Proposal

Independent assessments point to the same gap. The 2024 Joint External Evaluation (JEE) scored Kenya at:

JEE indicatorScore (1–5)Why, according to the JEE
Early warning surveillance3Indicator- and event-based surveillance not fully integrated; low EBS coverage.
Analysis and information sharing3No cross-sector data-sharing framework; animal-sector data shared only quarterly.
Activation and coordination of health personnel in an emergency1Lowest score in the whole evaluation.

The consequence runs through one mechanism: because signals from different systems are never joined into a single record of the same event, escalation depends on manual reconstruction, detection is delinked from response, and the time between them cannot even be measured consistently. Proposal

Why the decision side matters too

Even after a signal is verified, escalation has historically relied on informal discussion and individual experience. KNPHI addressed this with the Decision-Making Tool for Public Health Emergencies (DMT-PHE) — validated at a national workshop in October 2025 through five simulation exercises (Ebola, cholera, RVF, chemical spill, unknown aetiology) and launched in March 2026. Research But DMT-PHE is only as good as the information manually assembled for it. RADAR's job is to feed it automatically.

What RADAR is — and is not

RADAR is…RADAR is not…
An operational layer embedded in existing EOC workflowsA new national database or a replacement for KHIS2, KABS, ADaM or any other system
A shared Minimum Event Record that gives one event one identity across systemsA system every institution must log into and work inside
Risk scoring and anomaly detection across human, animal and environmental streamsAn autonomous decision-maker — a named officer confirms or overrides every recommendation
A digitised DMT-PHE with an AI assistant that cites its sourcesA general chatbot; the agent only works inside the DMT-PHE workflow
Disease-agnostic: reconfigured by changing thresholds, weights and pathwaysA one-disease tool needing a rebuild for each new threat

How it works in one picture

The proposal describes four building blocks Proposal:

  1. Bridge — the Minimum Event Record (MER). A small, standard set of fields and timestamps that each system can publish about an event. Achieves “systems integration” before heavy infrastructure exists. → doc 03
  2. Multi-stream integration and intelligence layer. Ingests and harmonises IDSR, EBS, KABS, EarthRanger, KMD, NDMA and contextual data; uses NLP and graph-based models for semantic translation, anomaly detection and early signal fusion. → docs 05 and 06
  3. Decision-support layer — DMT-PHE. Predefined trigger thresholds and escalation pathways aligned with IHR (2005) Annex 2 and 7-1-7. → doc 07
  4. Agentic AI layer. An assistant on top of DMT-PHE that compresses the detection-to-action loop, with citations, reasoning traces and human confirmation. → doc 07

Four outbreak categories, one workflow

RADAR is disease-agnostic in architecture but starts with four high-impact categories, each using the same detection–escalation workflow Proposal.

CategoryExamplesStreams that matter most Recommended
Enteric & diarrhoealCholera, typhoid, E. coli, other waterborne threatsIDSR/KHIS2, EBS, lab, rainfall & flooding, WASH context
Viral haemorrhagic feversRift Valley fever, Bundibugyo Ebola, Marburg, yellow feverEBS, KABS (livestock abortions), wildlife, climate, cross-border signals
Febrile & respiratoryInfluenza, mpox, other systemic syndromesIDSR, EBS, sentinel and lab data, media (EIOS)
Unknown aetiology (PHEIUE)Clusters of illness or death with no confirmed causeAll streams — early fusion speeds hypothesis generation before a pathogen is known

Two operating modes. In rapid-response mode RADAR is brought to immediate readiness to support an active high-consequence outbreak (the proposal cites the Bundibugyo Ebola situation in East Africa — declared a PHEIC by WHO in May 2026 Research). In continuous-monitoring mode the same architecture watches endemic and outbreak-prone diseases.

The proposal names four outcome measures; the stage gates turn them into thresholds (doc 13) and the MEL framework defines how each is measured (doc 14).

Evidence so far

  • Data systems mapped with KNPHI for integration. Proposal
  • Prototype AI-enabled DMT-PHE built and evaluated with 11 surveillance professionals (March 2026): mean concordance 0.924 against the DMT-PHE reference, SUS 85.2 (“excellent”), and no true adverse decision risks across 33 simulated interactions in three outbreak scenarios. A prototype is online at dmt-agent.streamlit.app. Proposal
  • These are simulation results from a small group. The gates exist to show the same performance holds under real workload, at every site, on live data. → doc 12

Design principles

Complement, not replace

Existing systems stay authoritative for their own data.

Open standards

FHIR R4, Kenya Core and MCP, so new streams plug in without a rebuild.

Incremental & modular

Start with high-value events and sources, prove value, then expand.

Government stewardship

KNPHI owns and runs it; hosting follows DHA requirements.

EOC-embedded

Built into the escalation workflow officers already use.

Human in the loop

Every recommendation is confirmed or overridden, with a logged reason.
