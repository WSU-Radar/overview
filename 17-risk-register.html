Risk Register

The main delivery, technical, data, AI and sustainability risks, how likely and serious each is, and how each will be reduced.

For: Leadership & funderEngineeringGovernance & legalStatus: Draft v0.1Updated: 29 October 2026

In short

  • Fifteen risks across delivery, data, technology, AI, adoption and sustainability, each with a likelihood, impact, mitigation and owner.
  • The highest-rated: slow data-sharing agreements (R1), weak source data or APIs (R2), low adoption (R3), procurement delays (R6), county power and connectivity (R8), staff turnover (R9) and no recurrent budget (R10).
  • Risks are reviewed at every steering committee and at each gate.

Why it matters

Most risks that would sink RADAR are not technical — they are agreements, people, procurement and money. Naming them early, with an owner and an early-warning sign, turns surprises at a gate review into issues handled months earlier.

3How risks are managed

  1. Review rhythm. Top risks at every TWG; full register at every steering committee and in each gate evidence pack.
  2. Escalation. Any risk rated High/High, or any early-warning sign that fires, goes to the steering committee within two weeks.
  3. Issues vs. risks. When a risk happens it becomes an issue with an action plan and date; the register records the outcome.
  4. Gate link. If a risk threatens the MVP deadline, the 30-day remediation notice to the foundation applies (doc 13).

4Key assumptions

If any of these turns out false, revisit the plan.

  • KNPHI can nominate a system owner, data stewards and ICT staff from the start.
  • At least four MVP sources can share data at Level 1–2 by March 2027.
  • KNPHI/DHA will authorise a production hosting location by mid-2027.
  • The DMT-PHE trigger-card set is stable enough to encode for the MVP.
  • Sobek can provide a GHEC-AI interface for joint specification in the MVP phase.
