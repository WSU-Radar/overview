Start Here: RADAR Documentation Map

How the document set is organised, how the documents depend on each other, and which ones to read first for your role.

For: Leadership & funderEngineeringEOC & surveillanceM&E / researchGovernance & legalStatus: Draft v0.1Updated: 29 October 2026

In short

  • The RADAR project is broken into 18 focused documents, each covering one area, workflow or decision.
  • They are grouped into five tracks: Orientation, Product & Architecture, Operations, Trust & Safety, Delivery, plus a glossary.
  • Every document opens with a plain-language summary and a “How this document connects” panel showing what it depends on and what it feeds.
  • Pick the reading path for your role below, or follow the map.

Why it matters

RADAR touches surveillance officers, engineers, lawyers, evaluators and funders. One long document would bury each group's questions inside everyone else's. Separate, linked documents let each reader go straight to what they own — while the links make sure no one designs a piece in isolation.

Grant start is 1 November 2026; these documents are the working baseline for kick-off.

1The document map

Read the map from top to bottom. The problem and landscape decide what the shared event record must hold; the build documents turn that record into a working system; operations put it into EOCs; trust-and-safety documents set limits on everything above; and the delivery documents turn the evidence into gate decisions.

5How to read and maintain these documents

Labels you will see

LabelMeaningHow to treat it
ProposalStated in the funded proposal (INV-110654), the stage-gate paper or the project deck.Committed scope. Change only through the consortium and foundation.
ResearchExternal evidence — laws, standards, published studies, vendor specifications.Check the cited source for the latest version before relying on it.
RecommendedA design choice proposed in these documents.A starting point for the technical working group to confirm or replace.
OpenA decision that has not yet been made.Needs an owner and a date. Each document ends with its open questions.

Keeping the set healthy

  • One owner per document. Suggested owners are listed in 15 · Partners & Governance.
  • Change the source, then the dependants. If the MER fields change (doc 03), revisit 04, 05, 06, 08 and 14.
  • Version and date every change. The header of each document shows its status and last update.
  • Close open questions in writing. When a Open item is decided, record the decision, the date and who made it, and change the label.
Inconsistencies found across the source documents

While breaking the project down, a few differences between the proposal, the stage-gate paper and the deck came to light — for example five vs. four county EOCs in Phase 2, a 0.85 vs. 0.88 concordance threshold at Gate 2, and budget line items that add up to more than the total. Each is flagged where it matters (mainly in documents 13 and 16) and should be settled at kick-off.
